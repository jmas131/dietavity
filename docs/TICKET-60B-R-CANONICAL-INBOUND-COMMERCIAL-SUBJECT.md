# Ticket 60B-R — Canonical inbound and commercial authorization subjects

`ConversationContext.current_message.canonical_message_id` is the UUID of the reserved canonical inbound `public.messages` row. It identifies one durable inbound ledger message across retry and recovery. It is distinct from `source_id`, a provider message ID, session/context identity, and a workflow attempt.

Only `commercial_purchase_intent` carries the deterministic, model-independent pair `authorization_subject_version: commercial-authorization-subject-v1` and `authorization_subject_id`. The interpreter adds it only after validating model output. Its SHA-256 input is the explicitly ordered tuple `[version, commercial_purchase_intent, campaign, offer_version]`; it contains no inbound identity, obligation ID, order, evidence, confidence, raw text, timestamp, or model metadata. Ticket 62 recomputes the subject from validated context and rejects a mismatch. The subject identifies commercial semantics, not an authorization, execution, or delivery event.

The current closed commercial distinctions are campaign (`standard` versus `glp1_validation`) and `offer_version`: campaign selects checkout versus holding-page authorization, while offer version scopes the commercial offering. Exact duplicate interpreter records retain the existing semantic duplicate normalization. If non-identical interpreter records nevertheless carry the same commercial subject, Ticket 62 rejects the collection fail-closed rather than authorize two side effects. Unrelated obligations and output order do not change the subject. Different inbound messages intentionally retain the same subject when their commercial semantics match; a future Ticket 59B combines this subject with the canonical inbound identity.

`reminder_request` remains a recognized interpreter category. The v2 launch policy maps explicit reminder intent to deterministic `unsupported` policy handling (`reminder_scheduling_unavailable`) with `reminder_scheduling_claim` prohibited. It creates no `schedule_reminder` action, even when legacy `reminderContexts` is supplied. Shared `schedule_reminder` vocabulary and Ticket 59A timestamp validation remain for compatibility and a future explicitly scoped reminder subsystem; vocabulary presence is not launch authorization.

The canonical ledger already reserves inbound rows under `inbound:<Twilio SID>` and exposes the durable `public.messages.id`. A v2 production adapter must supply that UUID as `canonical_message_id` before policy authorization; this ticket does not change the production workflow or persistence lifecycle.
